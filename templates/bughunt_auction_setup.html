Bug Hunt Auction

Teams bid points on how confident they are, then hunt the planted bugs against the clock. Runs entirely on the board — students don't need devices.

The Flow

  1. Setup: Show the code snippet on the board. Teams have time to read and understand it.
  2. Bidding: Each team bids points (from their current pool) on how confident they are to find all the bugs.
  3. Hunting: The highest bidder gets the hunt attempt. They have a fixed time limit to identify every bug.
  4. Scoring: If they find all bugs → they win the pot. If they miss any → their bid goes into the pot.
  5. Pot grows: Each round's planted bugs are worth 20 points × bug count to the pot. Losing bids also feed it.

Why Bid? The Strategy

Bidding does two things:

  • Risky bet: Bid high if your team is confident (you gain the pot on success). Bid low if unsure (you protect points).
  • Defensive move: Even a low bid blocks other teams from hunting that round. Sometimes it's worth the risk.

Example Round (4 bugs, 5-bug pot, 120 second hunt)

Pot: 80 points (from previous rounds) Bugs worth: 4 × 20 = 80 points Team Alpha bids 30 points Team Beta bids 50 points ← highest bid (hunts) Team Gamma bids 10 points Beta hunts for 120 seconds... → Beta finds 3 bugs but misses 1 Result: • Beta loses their 50-point bid → pot becomes 80 + 50 = 130 • Alpha and Gamma keep their bids (they weren't attempting) • Next round: pot = 130, ready to grow again

If Beta Had Won

Beta finds all 4 bugs in time Result: • Beta wins the entire pot (130 points!) • Pot resets to 0 for the next round

Teacher Tips

  • Show team points and the pot on the board the whole time — it builds tension.
  • Hunt time should be 60–180 seconds depending on complexity. Start with 120.
  • Emphasize all bugs must be found — missing one costs the bid.
  • If a team bids 0, they skip that round. You can still move to the next round without running out of bidders.
